# Market and differentiation (Phase 2)

> Part of the problem-first product build. Phase 1 (the problem) lives in
> `01-problem-and-product-definition.md`. This is Phase 2: the landscape, the gap, and the one
> reason someone switches.
> **Written:** 2026-08-07

## Who else solves this (the landscape)

| Tool | What it is | What it does well | What's missing |
|---|---|---|---|
| **SEEK** | Australia's main job board, plus a profile | Discovery and reach, near-monopoly on AU jobs | Doesn't remember you across applications, no help deciding what's worth it, not joined up, a walled garden |
| **LinkedIn** | Job board, profile and network | Reach, professional identity, recruiter marketplace | Works for recruiters as much as for you, forgets you each application, not built for AU contracting |
| **Indeed / Jora** | Aggregator job boards | Volume of listings | Same as above: no memory, no decision help, not joined up |
| **ChatGPT (what you use now)** | General AI chat | Cheap, fast tailoring | No memory, forgets you every time, not connected to search or tracking |
| **Spreadsheets / Notion** | Manual trackers | Free, flexible | All manual, no scoring, no writing help, no memory |
| **Teal / Huntr / Rezi / Canva** | Resume and tracker tools | Tidy single-purpose tools | Each does one slice only, not end-to-end, not AU-contractor-aware, don't tell you what's worth applying for |

## The gap (real demand, not just an empty slot)

Nobody joins the **whole process** with a **memory of you**, and nobody is built for the
**Australian contractor**. This is real demand, not just absence of supply: the pain is lived,
recurring every 6 to 12 months, and the people with it (contractors) have money. An empty slot
with no demand would be a trap; this slot has a paying, hurting audience.

## Differentiation: why someone switches

**The lead reason: it remembers you.**

> A Brisbane IT contractor switches from ChatGPT, spreadsheets and SEEK to this because **it
> remembers them, so every application is faster and sounds like them, instead of starting from
> scratch every time.**

Secondary edges (they support the lead, they don't replace it):

- It tells you which jobs are actually worth applying for, so you stop wasting hours on ghost
  jobs and bad fits.
- The whole process is in one place, not five disconnected tools.
- It's built for Australian contracting: clearances, Authority to Represent, PAYG vs ABN,
  suitability statements. Nobody else is.

## Who we say no to (for now, not forever)

- **Graduates and juniors.** Little history to remember, low ability to pay.
- **Agencies and employers.** We build for the job seeker, never the other side of the table.
  Of the three, this is the one most likely to stay a no, and if it's ever revisited it's only
  with clear eyes about the conflict it creates.
- **Non-Australian markets.** Australia only to start.

**Not on the no list:** permanent job seekers. The wedge is contractors, but the app already
handles contract vs permanent, so permanent roles are welcome.

**Important:** every "no" above is a **"not now," not a "not ever."** The product has to be
built so it can scale later to other user types and markets. **Scalability is a stated
requirement**, carried forward to the stack decisions (Phase 4).
